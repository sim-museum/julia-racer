# How to race in Julia Racer

You drive Jim Clark's 1967 Lotus 49: about 400 hp, narrow treaded tyres, no wings and no driver aids. It is
fast, light and nervous, and it rewards a smooth driver far more than a brave one. This guide gets you onto
the track, then teaches the driving that makes these cars quick.

> **Smoothness + balance = speed.** Every technique below is a way of keeping the car balanced
> and in your control. *(Jim Pearson, Classic Driving Course)*

---

## 1. Your first session

1. **Controller.** A wheel and pedals are best; a joystick or the keyboard work too. Plug the controller
   in and open the **Controller** tab. **Autodetect** (the default) finds the controller you plug in: a Thrustmaster TX
   or a Logitech Extreme 3D Pro works straight away; any other controller -- or to fine-tune yours -- press **Start**:
   centre and release everything, move every control through its full travel once, then use each control as the
   wizard asks (it finds them by their movement, and a wheel's quarter turn left measures its range). Each controller
   keeps its own calibration. **Saved profile** uses one profile -- built-in or one you calibrated -- whatever is
   plugged in.
2. **Race tab.** Pick a **Track** and **Session: Practice**. Leave the car on **reference default** and the
   gearbox on **Automatic** while you learn.
3. Press **Start practice**. The first start compiles and loads the game, and the window can take a few
   minutes to appear; later starts are quicker.
4. Drive ten easy laps. Learn where the track goes before you try to go fast.

When you can lap without spinning, switch the **Session** to **Race**.

## 2. Sessions and the start

* **Practice** puts you alone on the track. Use it to learn a circuit and your braking points.
* **Race** puts you on the grid against up to five AI cars for the number of laps you choose.
  * **Opponent pace** sets the field's speed as a percentage of the circuit's Classicrank reference lap.
    It presets itself from your own recorded laps, or 50 % if you have none. Start low and raise it
    as you improve. A close race teaches more than a lonely lead or a lonely last place.
  * **Qualifying session first** gives you a practice session whose best lap sets your grid slot. Press
    **T** (or **Enter**) to end it and go to the grid.
* **The start.** The field waits on the grid. Press **any key** when you are ready: a five-second
  countdown runs, then you go. The car cannot move before you press a key.

## 4. The car and its two setups

The Lotus 49 is the fastest car of 1967 and the least forgiving. The Cosworth DFV delivers its power in a
rush, and the chassis has no downforce to hold it down. Two setups are available, each fitted to reference
telemetry of the real car:

* **reference default** is the balanced base setup. **Learn on this one.**
* **WW103 fast loose** is Wolfgang Wagner's Watkins Glen setup: soft front springs, a stiff rear bar, a
  differential that locks under power, front toe-out and long gearing. It turns in sharply and is loose on
  power, but controllable at speed once you can catch a slide.

*A good balanced setup that you know beats a "fast" setup you are fighting.* The fastest Classic drivers report
getting within a second of their best times on the default setups. Change one thing at a time, and only
when you can say what you want it to fix.

## 5. Driving the line

The line is faster than the foot. For each corner, find three marks and hit them every lap:

1. **Braking point.** Brake in a straight line, heading for the turn-in point.
2. **Turn-in point**, almost always at the outside edge of the track. Braking should be nearly finished.
3. **Apex**, the inside point of the curve. Missing it by half a metre is missing it.

Then, as the corner opens, **unwind the steering and feed in the power together**, drifting out to the
far edge of the track at the exit.

**Slow in, fast out.** Before a long straight, the exit speed decides your lap time. A few km/h more at
the exit is worth far more than braking a few metres later. In long constant-radius corners, be patient:
hold a steady partial throttle and wait for the exit before you accelerate.

## 6. Braking

* **Brake progressively.** Squeeze the pedal on over a moment: the weight moves onto the front tyres and
  the car stays balanced. Then press harder all the way to the corner. Stamping on the brake at the last
  moment ("stab and release") locks the fronts and loses steering just when you need it.
* **Trail-brake** into the corner: keep a little brake pressure, easing off, as you turn in. It shortens
  the braking zone and helps the nose turn.
* **Downshift late and gently.** A downshift adds engine braking to the rear wheels only. On top of
  heavy braking, that can lock the rears and spin the car. Change down when the lower gear will engage
  well below its rev limit, and be careful into first and second.
* If the fronts lock (the car stops turning, the tyres squeal), **ease off the brake** until they roll again.

## 7. Throttle and gears

* **The throttle is not an on/off switch.** In 1967 the power overwhelms the tyres. Roll the throttle on
  and off progressively, as a motorcycle racer would.
* **Partial throttle** keeps the car balanced through long corners. Full throttle comes when the car is
  straightening.
* **Short-shift** (change up early) in the low gears when the rears spin, or when a shift would come in
  the middle of a tricky corner.
* In a manual car, **lift off as you change up** and squeeze the power back on after the gear engages.
* Avoid **downshifting at turn-in**. The jolt through the drive train can snap the rear loose. Do your
  downshifts in the braking zone.

## 8. Balance, sliding and the four-wheel drift

* **Understeer** (the car runs wide and won't turn): roll off the throttle to put weight on the fronts,
  and keep the steering moderate. More lock than the tyres can use only scrubs speed.
* **Oversteer** (the tail steps out): steer toward where you want to go (opposite lock) and roll off the
  throttle just enough to restore rear grip.
* **Last-resort save.** If a slide is past recovery by the normal methods, quickly **centre the steering**.
  On the limit, the fronts grip better pointed straight: in a big understeer you regain steering and
  braking, and in a spin the fronts slide with the rears so all four go sideways together. Do it only when
  everything else has failed.
* **Lifting off rotates the car.** A sharp lift moves weight forward and lets the tail come round. You can
  use that on purpose to point the car into a corner and then steer it on the throttle. A good place to
  learn is the fast dune corners at Zandvoort: go through just lifting, without braking.
* The goal is the **four-wheel drift**: the car sliding through the corner on all four tyres with the
  steering near centre and the throttle holding the angle. It is fast because the car is not scrubbing
  speed against big steering angles. Learn when **not** to use it, too: it is not the quickest way through
  every corner.

## 9. Racing the field

* **Race to finish.** Drive within yourself on the first laps; many who charge are off by the end of
  lap one.
* **Follow before you pass.** Sit a car length or more behind, drive a clean line, and wait for a mistake
  or a better exit.
* **Commit only with an overlap.** Go for a pass only when your front wheels are alongside the other
  driver by the turn-in point.
* **Defend fairly.** Changing your line more than once to block is not racing. If you take the inside to
  defend, brake earlier: you are off your normal turn-in point.
* **After a spin**, Shift + R puts you back on the track where you are. Rejoin carefully, out of the way
  of cars at racing speed.

## 10. Getting faster

* **Consistency first.** Aim for five laps within a second of each other before chasing a best lap.
  Speed comes from repeating good laps, and many drivers set their best when they relax and drive smoothly.
* **Learn one track and the car well** before moving on. The marks you learn at one circuit teach you the
  car everywhere.
* **Watch your replays.** After a race, open the **Replays** tab and watch your laps from the outside
  (V changes the camera angle, C changes the car). Watch the cars that beat you through the same corners.
  A replay of your hardest stop shows whether the fronts or the rears lock first.
* **Practice braking.** On a long straight, see how short you can stop from top speed without locking,
  changing down as you go.

### Circuit notes

* **Zandvoort.** Tarzan, the hairpin after the main straight, rewards a straight-line stop and a patient
  exit. The fast corners through the dunes are where to learn to rotate the car on a lift.
* **Watkins Glen.** Flowing and quick: the Esses and the Big Bend reward a smooth line and confidence on
  the throttle.
* **Monza.** Long straights and heavy braking: Curva Grande, the two Lesmos (aim inside the apex, as the
  car drifts away) and the Parabolica, where exit speed onto the main straight decides the lap.
* **Spa.** Very fast and unforgiving: Burnenville and the Masta kink need commitment and a steady hand.
  Aim inside the apex where the car drifts wide.
* **Nürburgring.** About 23 km of blind crests and changing corners. Learn it section by section (the
  launcher can show each section's name) before you try to go quickly.
* **Skidpad (test area).** A safe place to feel the car's limits, practise catching slides, and try the
  two setups back to back.
