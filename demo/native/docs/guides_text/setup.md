# Setting up the car

## How to work

- **Start from the base setup.** It is good. Before you change anything, drive until your lap times are repeatable;
  otherwise you can't tell whether a change helped.
- **One change at a time**, and big enough to feel (two clicks or more). If it went too far, go back one click.
- **Every change has a cost.** Grip gained at one end, or in one part of the corner, is usually lost somewhere else.
- **Set up for the corners that matter**: the fastest one, and the one onto the longest straight.
- **Keep it symmetrical** left to right, unless you know why not.
- **Write it down**, or use Save as… in the Garage, so you can go back.

## Understeer and oversteer

- **Understeer:** the front runs out of grip first. You turn the wheel and the car runs wide, nose first.
- **Oversteer:** the rear runs out of grip first. The tail steps out; too much and the car spins.
- **Neutral:** both ends let go together.

Say *where* it happens: on the brakes, at turn-in, mid-corner, or on the power at the exit. Each part of the corner has
its own cures (see **Fixing a problem** below).

## TIRES

**Cold pressure.** Higher pressure stiffens the tyre: it responds faster at turn-in but grips less over bumps and kerbs.
Lower pressure grips better but feels slow to respond. A heavily loaded tyre (compressions, banking) wants more
pressure. To shift the balance: lower the front pressures (or raise the rears) for more front grip, and the reverse
for more rear grip.

## CHASSIS

**Spring perch offset** sets that corner's ride height and preload. A bigger offset means less preload: the corner sits
lower and carries less weight. Change both sides of an axle together to change the ride height without moving the
corner weights. Watch the **ride height** (too low and the car bottoms out in compressions and under braking) and the
**shock deflection** (keep travel in hand). Rake is front lower than rear.

**Spring rate.** Stiffer front springs give more stability and more understeer; softer gives more front grip but less
stability. Stiffer rear springs reduce understeer mid-corner and at the exit; softer gives more rear grip and more
understeer.

**Packer.** A spacer that makes the bump rubber come in sooner, which limits travel. Use it to stop the car bottoming
out without stiffening the springs.

**Bump and rebound stiffness** (dampers). They don't change how much weight moves; they change how *fast* it moves, so
they act in the transitions: braking, turn-in, the brake release and the first throttle.
- **Front bump** stiffer: slows the dive under braking, at a little cost in grip over bumps.
- **Rear bump** stiffer: less understeer at turn-in and on the power, but risks oversteer. Softer: better traction and
  more understeer.
- **Front rebound** stiffer: a sharper turn-in as you release the brakes; too much gives oversteer on entry.
- **Rear rebound** stiffer: more understeer on entry. Softer: less entry understeer and better traction.

**Camber** (the top of the tyre tilted inward is negative). More negative camber keeps the contact patch flat when the
car rolls, which gives more mid-corner grip. It costs grip in a straight line: the tyres lock earlier under braking.
Too much gives a sudden slide at the front and snap oversteer at the rear. The camber shown follows the ride height:
lowering a corner adds negative camber.

**Toe-in.** The front tyres pointed slightly inward steady the car under braking and soften the first turn of the wheel.
Toe-out makes it turn in sharper but less stable. At the rear, toe-in gives stability and grip; toe-out makes the car
oversteer and is only for an understeer you can't cure any other way. Any toe costs a little straight-line speed.

**Anti-roll bars (diameter and arms).** The diameter is the big step, the arm hole the fine one. A stiffer bar puts more
of the cornering load on the outside tyre at that end, so that end loses grip.
- **Front stiffer:** more stable, more understeer; worse over bumps. **Front softer:** more front grip, less
  understeer.
- **Rear stiffer:** less understeer from mid-corner to the exit; too stiff and the rear snaps. **Rear softer:** more
  traction at the exit, a gentler slide. Run the rear as soft as keeps the balance.
- Both stiffer: the same balance, less roll.

**Brake bias** (percentage at the front). More front bias is stable, but the rears do too little and the fronts lock
first, which leaves you sliding straight on. More rear bias brakes better up to a point; then the rears lock and the
car spins. Find where the fronts lock, then go back a step or two. A little rearward bias helps the car turn as you
release the brakes; if you trail-brake a lot, try one step rearward.

**Steering ratio.** A quicker ratio needs less steering-wheel angle.

**Fuel.** Each litre adds weight. Carry what the race needs, plus a little.

## DRIVETRAIN

**Gears.** The Garage shows each gear's top speed.
- **Top gear:** reach the rev limit just before braking at the end of the longest straight, with a little spare for the
  tow.
- **The others:** space them so you aren't changing gear mid-corner and so the slow exits are in a gear that pulls.
  Avoid a gear that runs out just before a corner, forcing a change up and straight back down.

**The differential** (clutch plates, preload, drive and coast ramps). A locked diff makes both rear wheels turn at the
same speed; an open one lets them differ. **More plates, more preload and a smaller ramp angle** all mean more locking.
The **drive ramp** works on the power, the **coast ramp** off it (only with your foot fully off the throttle).
- **On the power:** too little locking and the inside rear spins, wasting power; too much and the car pushes straight
  on, then snaps when both rears spin. Somewhere between, the inside wheel neither spins nor pushes.
- **Off the power:** too little locking and the car turns in by itself; too much and it won't turn in. A little more
  locking than on the power usually gives a calm entry.
- More locking is calmer but understeers; less turns better but oversteers.

## Fixing a problem

First check the driving: most problems are a line or a pedal, not the setup.

| Problem | Driving | Setup |
|---|---|---|
| Fronts lock under braking | Brake a little earlier and softer | Bias rearward |
| Rears lock, spin under braking | Brake in a straight line; change down later | Bias forward |
| Won't turn in | Slower entry; release the brake more gently | Softer front bar or springs; lower front pressure; stiffer front rebound; a little front toe-out |
| Spins at turn-in | Finish braking earlier; turn in smoothly | Softer rear bar; bias forward; more coast locking |
| Understeer mid-corner | Less speed, less steering lock | Softer front bar; more front negative camber; stiffer rear bar |
| Oversteer mid-corner | Smoother throttle; don't lift suddenly | Softer rear bar; stiffer front bar; more rear negative camber |
| Understeer on the power | Later apex; feed the throttle in | Stiffer rear bar; less drive locking; lower front pressure |
| Oversteer on the power | Ease into the throttle; straighten the wheel first | Softer rear bar or springs; more rear traction (lower rear pressure); less drive locking if both rears spin |
| Inside rear spins on exit | Less throttle until straighter | More plates or preload; smaller drive ramp |
| Bottoms out | Brake less hard over compressions | Raise the ride height; add packer; stiffer springs |
